## Interpretation and next evaluation after Claude-side checks

Assessment: evidence supports selective use and further targeted evaluation. It does not establish that orchestration cannot equal or reduce tokens/cost, nor that hours of elapsed time predict benefit. No invocation settings are changed by this assessment.

The first native extraction pair ran no helpers and used unequal tools and skill/plugin catalogs. Its first-context counts (8,011 baseline versus 20,151 workflow) establish a context difference; they do not isolate the contribution of the skill body or each capability. Additional context can recur on later turns, including cached reads, so its cost is not necessarily a one-time startup charge. Historical reviewer trials did include real review delegation, but showed no extra detection on a weak/disputed-truth task. Useful substantial Luna/Haiku delegation remains untested.

The current Claude explorer smoke record reports a $0.00050445 Haiku estimate within a $0.03129855 family estimate; Sonnet accounts for $0.0307941. This proves little about useful work: the child only replied OK. A cheap child does not make a parent-led family cheap automatically. Count dispatch, integration, checking and failures, and compare them with owner work actually omitted and rework avoided. Different model prices and caching mean fewer dollars and fewer raw tokens are distinct outcomes.

Primary evidence:

- [Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3) compares agent topologies across task classes under matched reasoning-token budgets. Results differ for naturally parallel work and sequential planning. Matching reasoning tokens does not match every input/cache token or prove lower total cost. This preprint supports task-structure dependence, not a universal rule or hours threshold.
- [DELEGATE-52: LLMs Corrupt Your Documents When You Delegate](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15597v1) studies repeated document-editing interactions and document integrity. It is not a multi-agent versus single-agent cost study. Its name does not justify a claim about lengthy subagent workflows.
- [Anthropic's research-system account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) describes quality and latency benefits from parallel research alongside substantially higher token use. Its chat-interaction token comparison is not a matched scientific coding benchmark.

Opt-in is a defensible provisional policy for optional orchestration, preserving project-required reviews and scientific gates. It does not establish zero overhead. [Claude's invocation control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills#control-who-invokes-a-skill) can remove a user-only skill's description from automatic context. [Codex's invocation policy](https://developers.openai.com/codex/skills#optional-metadata) prevents implicit selection; zero metadata/tool/agent overhead must be measured separately. Current Codex global guidance explicitly requires the skill for substantial scientific tasks; metadata alone would not remove that loading route. Preserve native agents and distinguish optional team coordination from always-required project authority/checks.

Prioritized evaluation:

1. Analyze a bounded set of existing successful and failed long sessions before new execution trials. Separate active reasoning/tool turns from waiting. Extract context growth, cached/new input, compactions, repeated reads, failed attempts, unnecessary reviews, drift, accepted artifacts and recovery. Use scripts for counts, then inspect only decision-relevant excerpts. This avoids new task-execution trials; interpretation still consumes model tokens. Historical associations identify hypotheses, not counterfactual savings.
2. Verify explicit invocation and routine non-invocation only if the policy changes. Reuse Claude's dispatch/accounting evidence; do not repeat five-role smoke calls. The prepared semantic pair remains a small integration/control diagnostic, not a long-task-benefit test.
3. Compare representative substantial independent retrieval and dependent implementation separately. Freeze accepted-result criteria, the capable single-owner/direct-tool baseline, allowed helper lane, source versions, review gates, accounting and budget before launches. Judge accepted quality, drift and recovery first, then cost/time per accepted outcome and their tradeoffs. Initial small comparisons are diagnostic; general defaults require held-out cases and justified repetition.

Proposed admission criterion: optional orchestration needs substantial independent work, useful isolation of irrelevant context, or a consequential review requirement. Duration alone is insufficient: hours waiting for a solver may need no helper, while a short independent investigation may benefit. Keep one capable owner for interdependent steps. Do not run another broad model matrix or an hours-long campaign merely to force a favorable savings result. Further live allocation and the unresolved budget constraint remain as recorded above.
